PrimeTime.reWatchability dataset

PrimeTime rates finished football, tennis, F1 and winter sports events from 0 to 10 for how entertaining they are to watch, without spoiling the result. This dataset holds {{TOTAL}} events with every data point behind each score, and the source of each value. Use it to study what makes a match worth watching.

Every event has its score, the heat strip (how the action is spread over the event, 0 to 3 per part), the skip advice, a line-by-line breakdown of the score and the result. {{N_FACTS}} football matches also have the raw match facts the score is computed from: exact goal minutes, pre-match win chances from the odds, xG, xG on target, big chances, box touches, shots per team, cards, penalties, VAR, woodwork, team strength and stakes.

Key fields

FieldWhat it is
primetime.scoreThe 0 to 10 watchability score: the thing to explain or predict.
primetime.breakdownEach scoring line's points and a plain-English label, e.g. +1.5 Within one goal for 75% of the match.
primetime.timelineHeat per part of the event. Football: six 15-minute blocks. F1: ten lap chunks.
primetime.skipAdvice: watch it all, highlights, or skip these minutes, laps or sets.
primetime.scoringVersionFormula version. Scores from different versions aren't directly comparable.
resultFinal score, goal list with minutes and scorers, substitutions; tennis set scores; F1 and winter podiums.
factsFootball only: the raw numbers above, per team where it applies.
sourcesField path to the source it came from, on every record.

Before you use it

  • The score comes from a hand-tuned formula, not from viewers. There are no independent "how entertaining was it" ratings in this data, so the score is a hypothesis to test, not ground truth.
  • Raw facts exist only for football from the last 30 days. For older events the numbers used are inside the breakdown labels ("3 goals", "Chances worth 2.2 xG").
  • {{N_REC}} events were recovered from the project's history after they left the live site. They were scored with the formula of their time.
  • Elo ratings are the latest at export time, not at kick-off.
  • The underlying data comes from free, unofficial APIs and belongs to those providers. Use it for research and non-commercial work, and credit the sources.
